## The format is decided: the existing literal, not a new one

The unified format is `cosmic.literal`, which the two `_build` floors already
use. The reasoning turns on one fact about that module: it **lexes and
matches, never loads** (`cosmic/literal.tl:1-3`). The objection that ruled it
out on first pass — that a Lua table literal cannot take a `merge=union`
safely, because two `[&#34;path&#34;] = N` entries both survive and last-wins picks
silently — is a property of Lua&#39;s evaluator, not of a parser. `parse_table`
walks tokens and writes `out[key] = value`; it is one branch away from seeing
the duplicate and deciding what to do about it.

So the merge-safety that made `.cosmic-coverage` a hand-rolled text format is
buildable in the reader the tree already ships and already tests, and the
unification costs a duplicate-key contract instead of a second parser and a
second format. Its layout is line-oriented already: `_literal_format` writes
one `[&#34;key&#34;] = value,` per line, sorted, and is a `cosmic --check fmt` fixpoint
by construction — which is exactly the property a union merge needs.

The cost, stated: `.cosmic-coverage` stops being awk-trivial. A row becomes
`[&#34;path&#34;] = {12, 40},` rather than `path 12 40`. It stays greppable per path,
and one reader for three floors is worth the field extraction.

## Non-goals

tree already has one. No new ratchets: three exist and three get unified; a
fourth is a later card. No change to what any gate MEASURES: the cast count,
the public surface set, and the coverage ratio keep their semantics and their
values, so no floor&#39;s numbers move except by reformatting. No change to how
`*_pin.tl` files are read beyond the duplicate-key contract, which refuses by
default and so cannot loosen a pin. The markdown-table family
(`_build/ratchet.tl` and the five tests over it) is a different mechanism with
no committed floor and is out of scope.
