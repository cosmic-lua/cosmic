## Goal

G8 — the flow system. A `request changes` verdict names concrete quoted gaps
(`skills/work/review.md:78-89`) and the same PR carries the fixes. Nothing
connects the two: a rework push can answer three of four gaps, substitute a
differently-shaped fix for the fourth, and assert it satisfied the ask — and the
divergence is discovered only by the next full planner round. That makes an
unstated divergence cost a whole review cycle, while a stated one costs a
paragraph.

- **PR #1209 round 3.** The 04:23Z verdict named two one-line fixes for the

- **PR #1209 round 4.** The 07:26Z verdict named three things: stage into a
  process-private temp dir via `fs.temp_dir` honouring `TEST_TMPDIR`; remove the
  copy when scoring finishes; close the basename collision. None was done, and
  `_eval/score.tl:20` and `:71` both assert the destination *is* &#34;process-private&#34;
  when `scored_root()` returns a fixed shared `/cosmic-eval-scored`
  — the doc comment&#39;s own next sentence calls it &#34;the same well-known,
  OS-reclaimed location&#34;, contradicting the claim three lines above it.

Worth stating because it shapes the fix: in both rounds the implementer may have
been *right* that the named fix was insufficient. Round 4&#39;s staging really is
load-bearing — the checks write fixtures into the workspace by design
(`_eval/checks/support.tl:202-213`, `json-cli.tl:25,30`,
`contained-task.tl:23-36`), so a bare absolute-path one-liner would still have
written into the committed tree. The defect is not disagreeing with the reviewer;
it is disagreeing silently. This countermeasure must make stating a divergence
cheap and skipping it impossible — never make diverging itself harder.

## Change (to be settled in refinement)

The shape, not yet chosen. A `request changes` verdict enumerates its gaps as an
ordered list in a form the tool can read, and `move N check` refuses until the
rework carries a per-gap answer — `closed` or `diverged ()` — for each.

This is the mirror image of #1202&#39;s `enable:` trailer and should copy its
mechanics rather than invent new ones: a line-anchored grammar parsed out of a
comment body, a pure predicate in `_work/verdict.tl` beside `blocks_check` and
`blocks_send_back`, and one guarded call site in `cmd_move`. #1202 also
establishes the precedent for which side of the move gets refused, and the same
reasoning applies here.

Refinement must settle two things it cannot assume:

1. **Who numbers the gaps.** Requiring the planner to write a machine-readable
   gap list changes how every verdict is authored, and a verdict that forgets the
   form would strand the card. The weaker alternative — require only a single
   rework comment per push that names each gap in prose — is not machine-checkable
   and is what already fails today. Measure which is affordable before picking.
2. **What happens to the historical verdicts.** #1202 settled this for its own
   gate by reading the STANDING verdict only and leaving `--force` as the repair
   path; the same rule is the likely answer and should be stated, not inherited by
   assumption.

**Capacity, which decides the layout:** `_work/verbs.tl` is at 495/500 on `main`
and both PR #1247 and PR #1248 independently take it to exactly 500, against a
hard cap. The predicate goes in a module with headroom and only its call site in
`verbs.tl`. Re-measure after those two land.

## Non-goals

No change to the three verdicts, their meanings, or the `work-verdict:`
first-line grammar. No fourth verdict. Do not make diverging from a named fix
harder or discouraged — the goal is that a divergence is *stated*, and a
`diverged ()` answer must be as easy to write as `closed`. No judgement of
whether an answer is correct; presence and shape only, like #1202&#39;s trailer.
No retroactive enforcement on verdicts already posted.
